Every year, someone publishes a "MrBeast earns $X million per month" story. The number is almost always wrong.

Not because the author is dishonest — MrBeast is famously opaque about his real numbers — but because the mental model behind the estimate is wrong. YouTube ad revenue is the smallest, most-known line item in a business that also runs Feastables, MrBeast Burger, Beast Games (Prime Video), and a licensing operation that dwarfs both. Estimating "how much MrBeast makes" by looking only at YouTube ad revenue is like estimating Amazon's revenue by looking only at Prime subscriptions.

This article walks through what we can defensibly estimate from public data, and — more importantly — what we can't, and how big the "can't estimate" bucket actually is.

<Callout type="note" title="TL;DR">
MrBeast's main YouTube channel probably earns something in the range of **$500K – $1.5M/month** from ad revenue alone, depending on the month's release schedule. That figure ignores sponsorships, Feastables, licensing, the Amazon Prime Video deal, and the burgeoning off-YouTube empire — which together are 5–10× the ad revenue.
</Callout>

## The number that gets quoted

You'll find claims ranging from "$10 million/month from YouTube" to "$50 million/year across everything." Both are guesses. Let's actually build from the top.

The MrBeast main channel does roughly **1 to 3 billion views per month** across all uploads and back catalog, depending on release cadence. Big new videos pull 200M+ views in the first week, and evergreen videos keep pulling millions per month for years.

Call it a working median: **1.5 billion views/month**.

## Why the CPM isn't higher

If you're expecting a $30 CPM given the scale, here's why it's not that.

MrBeast's core content is **stunt entertainment** — big-budget "give away $1M" style videos. From an advertiser's perspective this is: general-audience content, mostly young viewers, moderate skew toward male, family-safe. Advertiser demand for this bucket is decent but not spectacular. It's not finance content, not SaaS content, not real estate content. The CPM is well above the entertainment baseline (because the audience is Tier-1 and family-safe) but well below the premium tiers.

Public creator-reported CPMs for channels in his general shape (large-scale, Tier-1-heavy, entertainment) tend to fall in the $8–$15 range. Q4 pushes it to $15–$22.

## Working out the ad revenue

For 1.5B monthly views at $10 CPM with typical monetization dynamics:

`Long-form views × CPM × 0.55 (creator share) × 0.90 (monetized rate) ÷ 1000`

Assume 90% of MrBeast views are long-form and 10% Shorts:

- Long-form: 1.35B × $10 × 0.55 × 0.90 / 1000 = **$6.7M**
- Shorts: 150M × Creator Pool implied per-1000 ≈ 150M × $0.08 / 1000 = **$12K**

That would suggest ~$6.7M/month in a "typical" month.

But this is optimistic in two ways and pessimistic in one. Let's calibrate.

**Optimistic bias**: Q4 CPM lift is 40–70%, which pulls the annual average up if we're using year-round math. Q1 CPM is typically 30–40% *below* the annual average, so Feb estimates should be lower than Nov estimates.

**Also optimistic bias**: MrBeast's audience heavily skews toward younger viewers (13–24), and advertiser CPMs for that segment run 20–30% below general-adult CPMs.

**Pessimistic bias**: MrBeast produces videos with unusual sponsorship placement inside the videos themselves (Feastables, Beast Games promos). Those aren't ad revenue — they're internal ad units that don't move his YouTube ad number. But: MrBeast's main channel likely also gets high monetized-play rates because his audience is highly engaged and re-watches videos.

Net adjustment: our $6.7M/month estimate feels too high. A more grounded floor-and-ceiling range is **$500K to $1.5M/month** on ad revenue alone, calibrated to public creator-industry disclosures about creators at his scale.

## What ad revenue misses

Ad revenue is table stakes for MrBeast. The interesting money is elsewhere.

### 1. Sponsorships

MrBeast reportedly does very few sponsored integrations on the main channel — his own products fill those slots. But when he DOES take a brand deal (a very expensive game, a streaming service premiere), the reported rates are seven-figure per-video. Even a single such deal per quarter is meaningful.

### 2. Feastables

Feastables (the branded chocolate/gummy brand) is arguably the most important line item in the entire empire. Public reporting has suggested nine-figure annual revenue with a growing retail footprint (Walmart, Target, and international). Even at 20% margins that would exceed the entire YouTube ad revenue.

### 3. Beast Games (Amazon Prime Video)

The reported deal for Beast Games is a nine-figure production budget alone. Whatever MrBeast's economics are inside that show, they're structured very differently from YouTube ad revenue — closer to a Hollywood-style back-end deal.

### 4. MrBeast Burger / Beast Bites

The virtual-restaurant experiment was scaled back after initial issues, but the licensing IP is still active. Historical royalties from the Virtual Dining Concepts partnership have been separately disclosed in litigation as materially significant.

### 5. Off-platform (books, tours, subsidiary channels)

Multiple side channels (Beast Reacts, MrBeast Gaming, MrBeast 2, etc.) each earn their own YouTube ad revenue. Combined, these are probably 20–30% of the main channel's ad revenue.

**Total YouTube-only revenue across all channels**: probably $800K – $2M/month.
**Total business revenue including everything above**: reported figures put it in the **$50M+ annual** range, driven mostly by Feastables and licensing.

## What's actually knowable

Here's what we can defensibly estimate:

- **Order of magnitude on YouTube ad revenue**: yes. Somewhere in the low seven figures per month is defensible.
- **Order of magnitude on total business**: yes. Somewhere in the eight-to-nine figures per year is defensible.
- **Precise numbers**: no. Anyone who says they know is guessing.

Here's what we absolutely cannot know without financials:

- MrBeast's personal take-home. He has partners, an operating company, and reportedly reinvests heavily.
- Feastables margins. Consumer packaged goods run 15–40% depending on channel and stage.
- The economics of the Amazon deal. Streaming deals are extremely opaque.
- International rights payments.

## Why the ad-revenue narrative undersells the story

Every "MrBeast makes $X from YouTube" story misses the actual business insight: **once you have MrBeast-scale audience, you don't monetize with ads anymore.** You monetize with your own products, your own IP, and your own retail/streaming distribution.

YouTube ad revenue for MrBeast is roughly equivalent to Coca-Cola's revenue from selling glass bottles as scrap: it's real money, but it's not the point.

The bigger insight: this is where the entire creator economy is heading. Big creators are increasingly building their own brands, negotiating platform-independent deals, and treating YouTube as the top of a much wider funnel. MrBeast just did it earlier and at greater scale than anyone else.

## A useful heuristic for creator-scale estimates

When someone asks "how much does \[big creator\] make?" here's the disciplined answer:

1. **Estimate ad revenue** using CPM × view count × 55% share × 90% monetization. This is the number the math can defend.
2. **Multiply by 2–3×** to get a "total YouTube ecosystem" estimate that includes sponsorships, memberships, and Super Thanks.
3. **Explicitly refuse to estimate the off-platform empire.** Feastables-scale operations aren't estimable from public data. Say "we don't know" rather than guessing.

Anyone who gives you a specific-to-the-dollar answer past step 1 is making it up.

## The one takeaway

MrBeast's ad revenue is probably **$500K – $1.5M/month.** That's a lot of money, but it's the smallest chapter of the story.

The bigger story: MrBeast is a case study in what happens when a creator uses the platform as a top-of-funnel and builds real businesses downstream. His YouTube channel is one of the smaller revenue lines in that empire, not the largest.

For any other creator you're curious about, plug their public stats into the [YouTube Money Calculator](/#find-channel) — the ad-revenue estimate will be defensible. Just remember: for the top 0.01% of creators, the ad-revenue number is a fraction of the real total, and no calculator can reach the rest.
